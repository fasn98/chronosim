## Abstract (≤ ~200 words)

- **What:** an independent re-implementation of the semiclassical calculation in Kim & Thorne (1991) of the renormalized stress-energy of a conformally coupled massless scalar field near the Cauchy horizon of a two-mouth wormhole time machine.
- **What was reproduced:** the closed-form results (eqs. 51 and 67), their Δ⁻³ divergence and sign change, the covering-space geometry, the defocusing factors, the polarized hypersurfaces and the four-momentum.
- **Discrepancies, stated neutrally:**
  - The point-splitting operator as printed (eq. 4a) lacks a factor ½ relative to the convention that reproduces a thermal state and the Unruh value.
  - The printed results carry that convention consistently.
  - Eqs. 31 and 52 and the value after eq. 14 are inconsistent with their own context.
  - None of these changes the qualitative conclusion.
- **Method:** provenance by construction.

## 1 Introduction

- **The original.** Kim & Thorne ask whether vacuum polarization near the Cauchy horizon of a wormhole time machine grows enough to destroy it before closed timelike curves form (title question; abstract, p. 3929).
  - Their answer: ⟨T^μν⟩ diverges as Δ⁻³ (eqs. 51 and 67, pp. 3943, 3945).
  - They argue that quantum gravity plausibly cuts the divergence off at Δt ~ l_P, so the growth is too small to prevent the time machine (eq. 9c and text, pp. 3934–3937; eqs. 13 and 14, p. 3935).
- **Why it matters.** Hawking (1992), the paper that states the chronology protection conjecture, disputes the cutoff (p. 603 and section VI). He argues that it should be in invariant distance, D Δt ~ l_P², where δg^VP reaches order 1.
  - Later rigorous work (Kay, Radzikowski & Wald 1997) shows that the two-point function cannot be Hadamard on a compactly generated Cauchy horizon, so the semiclassical question cannot be settled within the semiclassical framework.
- **What this replication does.**
  - It recomputes every closed-form result the paper derives from its own starting expressions (eqs. 20, 46, 50, 61, 66, and the geometry of eqs. 25 and 28), by numerical evaluation independent of the closed forms.
  - It checks the point-splitting operator against two references that do not depend on the paper.
  - It documents where the printed text and the computation disagree.
- **What it does not do:** decide between Kim & Thorne and Hawking. See section 6.

## 2 Methods

### 2.1 Independent re-implementation

- **Package:** `chronosim`, Python with mpmath arbitrary precision.
- **Starting points only.** The closed forms are not used to compute their own checks:
  - covering space by iterating the Poincaré map of eqs. 25 and 28;
  - finite-difference point splitting applied to eqs. 50 and 66;
  - geometric-optics transfer matrices for the defocusing;
  - root finding for the polarized hypersurfaces;
  - quadrature for the four-momentum.
- **Two operators in parallel throughout:** eq. 4a as printed, and eq. 4a with ½.
- **Units:** ħ = c = 1, with D = 1 unless stated.

### 2.2 Provenance by construction

- **Origins.** Every output is a `Quantity` with an origin: CITED (transcribed from a source), DERIVED (exact evaluation of CITED formulas), COMPUTED (any numerical procedure) or HEURISTIC (a value chosen without a source).
- **Degradation only.** Combination takes the worst origin, and there is no code path that promotes one.
- **Heuristics are visible.** Every HEURISTIC constant is registered and printed on every run (list from `outputs/report.md`, generated).

### 2.3 Anti-fabrication guard

- A test (`tests/test_anti_fabrication.py`) fails if any package module can reach a random number generator.
- Sampling is on deterministic grids only.

### 2.4 Transcription protocol for the scanned original

- The PDF of the original is a scan with no extractable text. Each equation used was transcribed in two separate passes:
  - pass 1 from crops at 250 dpi;
  - pass 2 by a separate model instance with no access to pass 1, from crops at 330 dpi.
- All 52 equations agree between the passes (table from `docs/equation_transcription.md`, generated).
- **Stated limitation:** both passes come from the same kind of model, so they are not two human readers. This is why the derivations of F3 and F4 also serve as a check.
- **Third, independent human reading** by the author:
  - eqs. 1, 3, 4a and 4b (2026-11-06);
  - eq. 31;
  - eq. 52 (2026-11-07);
  - the text after eq. 14 (2026-11-07).

### 2.5 Arbiters for the operator

- **Primary: thermal state in Minkowski space.**
  - The Hadamard function is the imaginary-time image sum derived from the KMS condition (`docs/thermal_images.md`), with the n = 0 term (the Minkowski vacuum) removed.
  - The reference is Stefan–Boltzmann for a massless scalar, π²T⁴/30, cross-checked by the Bose integral.
  - It depends on no closed article.
  - The image sum is also compared with a mode integral.
- **Secondary: Unruh effect in the Rindler vacuum.**
  - The reference ρ = −π²T⁴/30 with T = 1/(2πξ) is labelled **HEURISTIC**, because the relation is cited from Candelas & Deutsch (1977), which was not read (closed access; volume and pages confirmed in Crossref only).
- **Direct comparison:** eq. 4a against eq. 11 of Li & Gott (1998), on the Rindler function. Both papers normalize the Hadamard function identically (Kim & Thorne eqs. 3, 17, 20; Li & Gott eq. 9).

### 2.6 Validation of the method on Li & Gott (1998), phase 1

- **What is compared:** the image sum (Li & Gott eq. 8) with point splitting (eq. 11) against their closed form (eq. 12) for Misner space.
- **What is checked:**
  - agreement to numerical precision over a grid of ξ and b;
  - the ξ⁻⁴ horizon slope;
  - the vanishing at b = 2π.
- **Figures:** `fig1` (and `fig2` if space allows).
- **Source caveat:** Li & Gott was transcribed from arXiv gr-qc/9711074, not from the published PRL.

## 4 Discrepancies

Each item gives: what is printed (page, equation), what the computation shows, which references settle it, and what depends on it.

1. **Factor 2 in the point-splitting operator.**
   - Eq. 4a (p. 3933) as printed has no ½ relative to Li & Gott eq. 11, and no surrounding definition compensates for it (human check of eqs. 1, 3, 4a, 4b).
   - With ½, the thermal arbiter gives Stefan–Boltzmann and the Unruh arbiter agrees. Eq. 4a as printed gives twice both (generated ratios).
   - Eqs. 51 and 67 are reproduced **by the operator as printed**. The convention is carried consistently, so the printed values are a factor 2 above the convention that matches the arbiters.
   - **Literature:**
     - Klinkhammer (1992, PhD thesis, eqs. 3.3, 3.8, pp. 30–32) and Visser (hep-th/9303023, eqs. 10, 34) use the operator with ½. They obtain exactly half of eq. 67 (an algebraic identity, tested).
     - Both declare agreement with Kim & Thorne without commenting on the factor (quotes: thesis section 4, p. 38; Visser, after eq. 34).
     - No erratum was found (Crossref, APS page).
     - These transcriptions are single readings.
2. **Eq. 31 (p. 3940).**
   - As printed, its coefficient is inconsistent with Fig. 5 and with eqs. 25, 28 and 29.
   - The coefficient of opposite sign is consistent with all three (measured ratio −1, generated).
   - Only the slope distinguishes the two readings.
   - No later result depends on it.
3. **Eq. 52 (p. 3943).**
   - The radicand is printed as √(X²+Y²) (human check).
   - This is inconsistent with ρ = √(Y²+Z²) (eq. 39), with ρ̂ = ∂/∂ρ and the unit-vector description (p. 3943), and with eq. 51.
   - Eq. 51 is reproduced only with √(Y²+Z²). The deviation with the printed form does not fall with Δ (generated).
4. **The value after eq. 14 (p. 3935).**
   - The printed T⁰⁰_max ~ 10⁴⁹ g/cm³ is inconsistent with the expression (l_P/D)(m_P/l_P³) and the inputs printed alongside it (m_P ~ 10⁻⁵ g, p. 3943; l_P/D ~ 10⁻³⁵ with D = 1 m, p. 3935). Those inputs give the recomputed value (generated).
   - It is an order-of-magnitude estimate, and no conclusion depends on it.
   - Human check of the reading: the exponent is unambiguous.
5. **Minor:** the text after eq. 52 refers to "the stress-energy tensor (52)", meaning eq. 51. Mention in one line, or omit.

## 5 Discussion

- **No discrepancy changes the qualitative conclusion:**
  - the Δ⁻³ rate;
  - the sign change at the polarized hypersurface;
  - the location of the Kim & Thorne cutoff (Δt ~ l_P) and of Hawking's (D Δt ~ l_P²) in Fig. 4.
- **The factor 2 rescales magnitudes** by a constant and does not move an order-of-magnitude argument.
- **What the replication establishes:**
  - the closed forms follow from the paper's starting expressions;
  - the geometry and defocusing reproduce independently;
  - which operator convention matches the thermal and Unruh values.
- **What it does not establish:**
  - that the starting expressions (eqs. 20, 46, 50, 61, 66, geometric optics) are correct; they are inputs;
  - anything beyond a single conformal scalar on a fixed background with b ≪ D;
  - either side of the Kim & Thorne–Hawking dispute.
- **The question cannot be settled semiclassically:** Kay, Radzikowski & Wald (1997) show that the two-point function cannot be Hadamard on the horizon. The question needs quantum gravity.
- **Origin of the arbiters:** they anchor the operator in the thermal state and the Rindler vacuum, not in the wormhole.

## 6 Literature check

- **Question:** has anyone reported the factor, or is there an erratum?
- **Exact scope:**
  - 175 works citing Kim & Thorne on INSPIRE, of which 131 have an arXiv version; those 131 were downloaded as text and searched;
  - 99 of the 131 mention Kim & Thorne;
  - search terms: "factor of 2", "factor of two", "twice", "half", near Kim & Thorne in a T_μν context, and references to eqs. 4, 51 or 67;
  - Crossref and the APS page for errata;
  - web searches (list from `docs/literature_check_factor2.md`).
- **Not covered:**
  - 44 citing works without arXiv text;
  - mentions phrased differently, for example inside equations;
  - the published version of Klinkhammer (PRD 46, 3388).
- **Finding:** no report of the factor was found within that scope. The table is generated from the doc.

## 7 Limitations and pending items

- **Li & Gott (1998)** was transcribed from arXiv gr-qc/9711074. Comparison with the published PRL 80, 2980 is pending.
- **Candelas & Deutsch (1977)** was not read (closed access). The Unruh reference is therefore HEURISTIC, and the thermal arbiter is primary.
- **Birrell & Davies (1982)** was not read. The thermal derivation is written out in `docs/thermal_images.md` and does not depend on it.
- **Hiscock & Konkowski (1982)** is unavailable; the related phase is blocked and is outside this paper.
- **l_P** is DERIVED from the ratio l_P/D ~ 10⁻³⁵ with D = 1 m (p. 3935). The printed value 10⁻³³ cm (p. 3930) was not checked by the human reader.
- **Transcriptions:**
  - Klinkhammer and Visser were read once.
  - Kim & Thorne got two model passes, and the human reading covers only the equations listed in 2.4.
- **Parameter grids** are HEURISTIC and printed on every run.
